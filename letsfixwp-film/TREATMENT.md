# Back Online Today: treatment

Style: Tabletop App Flythrough. 45 s, 1920x1080, 30 fps, English voice-over, burned-in subtitles plus `.srt`.
For: the LetsFixWP home page, replacing `public/videos/what-a-fix-looks-like.mp4`.

## 1. Three structures

**A. One job travels the desk.** The client's broken site is a little pixel creature. It travels card to card
(message, price, backup, login, workbench) and comes back healthy, escorted by the team. Every stop is one of
our promises, so the route itself is "how we work".

**B. Two halves of the desk.** The left half is the owner panicking alone with a broken site, the right half is
the team working through it. Both meet at the end. Clear contrast, but half the screen time goes to the problem,
and the four promises get squeezed.

**C. A clock card counts the day.** A day-clock card in the middle ticks from morning to evening while the fix
happens around it. Strong "same day" message, but the clock competes with every station for attention and makes
the speed claim the hero rather than the safety of the process.

**Chosen: A.** Our visitor's fear is "what happens to my site once I hand it over?" A single journey answers that
in order, one promise per stop, and it gives the cast (three developers) a job at every station. The opening image
is the site already broken, not a logo. The ending mirrors it: the same browser card, now healthy, seen in the
closing pull-back over the whole desk.

## 2. Logline and arc

A small WordPress site breaks; the LetsFixWP team walks it around the desk (price agreed, backup taken, a login the
owner creates) and sets it back on its card, alive, the same day.

- Setup (0 to 8 s): the site collapses on its browser card; little visitors bump into the error and walk away.
- Turn (8 to 30 s): the team pops up, hears the problem, and walks the site through fixed price, backup and access.
- Ending (30 to 45 s): the fix (the one held silence, then the site snaps sharp and goes green), access removed,
  curtain call over the whole desk, title card drops: "Back online today."

## 3. Benchmark

- Headspace's animated explainers. Learn: a calm voice, one idea per line, warm palette, room to breathe between
  lines. Don't take: characters, colours, music, the round-blob design language.
- The Monument Valley launch trailer. Learn: one unbroken camera that glides, slow orbits, a final pull-back that
  reveals the whole world as a model. Don't take: architecture, geometry, score, any shot.

## 6. Cue map

- Tempo 120 BPM, 4/4, one key (D major), one chord per bar. Every pop, hop, stamp and ding on the 16th grid,
  stamps and the fix on downbeats.
- 0 to 8: marimba motif alone, sparse, shaker in at bar 2.
- 8 to 30: full bed (marimba, plucked bass, soft kick, shaker), ducked about 16 dB under the voice, lifted in gaps.
- 30 to 33: subtraction: kick and shaker drop out, only vibraphone pad.
- 33.0 to 33.8: near-silence (a room tone only).
- 34.0: downbeat: two-note ding plus the pop of the healthy site, then the full theme with claps.
- 39 to 45: curtain-call theme, each stop's hop a marimba note of the chord, final chord held to the last half second.

## 7. Sound design

| Section | Ambience | Foley | Music |
|---|---|---|---|
| Broken site 0 to 8 | quiet room, faint keyboard far off | glitch crackle, slump thump, two soft bumps | sparse marimba, minor-tinged |
| Team 8 to 17 | room | three pops (chord notes), typing clicks, bell flight + double tap | full bed, ducked |
| Promises 17 to 30 | room | stamp thump + crumble, drawer slide, window click, key pop, cross scratch | full bed, ducked |
| Fix 30 to 36 | room drops away | debris ticks, then silence, then ding + pop | pad only, then theme |
| Close 36 to 45 | room | key reverse-pop, hops (chord notes), card landing thump | curtain-call theme, final chord |

J-cut: the bell's flight sound starts before the whip to the phone lands (13 s). L-cut: the stamp's ring runs
over the move to the backup tower (21 s).

## 8. Type and subtitles

- In the world, only keywords: step badges (accent pill, digit and one word: "1 PRICE", "2 BACKUP", "3 ACCESS"),
  "CAUSE FOUND", "AGREED", "ONLINE", "ACCESS REMOVED", the error line on the browser card.
- Mono labels: JetBrains Mono. Sans for pills, titles and subtitles: Manrope 800. Both OFL, self-hosted.
- Subtitles: one cue per spoken line, single English line, bold about 40 px, ink box at 72 % opacity, 64 px above
  the bottom. Camera keeps subjects about 110 px above centre.
- Opening tent card beside the team: "LetsFixWP". Closing title card dropped into the open centre of the desk:
  "LetsFixWP" over "Broken WordPress? Back online today." and "letsfixwp.com".

## Facts used (all from the owner-confirmed list in PRODUCT.md)

Fixed price agreed before work starts; full backup before any change; work through a login the client creates,
never their password; access removed when done; "Back online today" (home H1). No statistics: the figures in
`brand.ts` are placeholders and stay out of the film.

line `#e0e4e8`, accent `#ff5a36` (LetsFixWP v4). Support: teal-blue `#2f8fb0`, plum `#8a5a9e`, cream `#f4e3b5`.
